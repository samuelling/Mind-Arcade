Adaptive Neuropsych Battery

Eleven short tasks, one Bayesian engine. Each next test is chosen by expected information gain while many patterns are live, then by how well it splits the top two once the field narrows. The session stops only when the leader is above 90% and two independent tests each favor it. Play the arcade version.

Takes 8 to 15 minutes. A keyboard works best; touch is supported.

    How the engine decides

    Pattern models

    Eight candidate patterns each predict a z-score on all 14 measures. Scores are treated as Gaussian around that prediction with SD σ = 0.7, conditionally independent given the pattern. The prior is flat, and the posterior is updated after every test.

    Explore

    For each remaining test, expected information gain EIG = H(P) − E[H(P | scores)] is computed by stratified Monte Carlo over the current posterior. EIG rewards tests that split many live patterns at once.

    Exploit

    For the leader a and runner-up b, D = Σ (μₐ − μ_b)² / 2σ² is the expected log-likelihood ratio a test will deliver between them. It rewards tests that settle the head-to-head.

    Blending

    The explore weight is w = (2^H − 1) / 7, the effective number of live patterns rescaled to 0–1. Utility is [w·EIGₙ + (1 − w)·Dₙ] / minutes, so the battery spends time where it buys the most certainty.

    Triangulation

    Each completed test is a sightline. It converges when its own scores favor the leader over the runner-up by BF ≥ 3. When the leader passes 90% on fewer than two sightlines, the engine switches to Confirm and prefers a test from a domain not yet converging.

    Stopping

    Converged: P ≥ 0.90 with two converging tests. Single-line: P ≥ 0.97 with nothing left that separates the top two. Unresolved: battery exhausted. Delayed recall unlocks two tests after list learning, and filler tests are chosen in between.
